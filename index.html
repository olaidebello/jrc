OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK

We don't schedule jobs. We plan successful operations.

A system that separates commercial control, operational execution, and communication — so the owner stops firefighting every job and starts running exceptions.

Lead→ Sales→ Proposal→ Follow-Up→ Booked Job→ Job Planning→ Crew Assignment→ Job Completion→ Owner Independence
01

Control — one source of truth

Three tools, three distinct jobs. None of them should overlap, and Slack should never become the system of record.

GHL

Commercial control

New Lead → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Follow-Up → Won/Booked → Lost. Tracks lead source, owner, proposal value, next action, booking status.

"Where does every opportunity stand?"

TRELLO

Operational control

Booked → Planning → Ready → Assigned → In Progress → Completed → Closed. Owns everything after the job is sold.

"What needs to happen to execute this job?"

SLACK

Communication

Urgent communication, exceptions, coordination, escalation, decisions in the moment.

Never the source of truth — just where people talk.

Plan — booked is not ready

04

Assign — right job, right crew

Not "who's available" — "who's capable of executing this specific job." Try the scenario below.

05

Execute — manage by exception

The Operations Manager doesn't chase every crew every day. The system surfaces where attention is actually needed.

Green

Crew confirmed, equipment ready, customer confirmed. Job proceeding normally — no action needed.

Amber

Crew Leader hasn't confirmed by deadline. Potential issue — needs a look before it becomes urgent.

Red

Crew cancellation with no backup identified. At risk — escalation required now.

08

Improve — fix the system, not the incident

When something goes wrong, the response is structural, not reactive.

Bad response

Find someone. The gap repeats next time.

Operational response

Find replacement → execute job → identify why there was no effective contingency → create backup requirement → monitor compliance.

09

Build the A-team

Authority moves progressively down the chain: Owner → Operations Manager → Crew Leaders → Crews. Each Crew Leader eventually owns their job end to end.

Crew Leader owns

  • Reviewing job info & scope
  • Preparing crew
  • Normal field decisions
  • Communicating issues
  • Completing & documenting the job

Ops Manager owns

  • The system and KPIs
  • Exceptions only — not micromanaging every crew

Owner owns

  • Strategic decisions
  • True escalations only
